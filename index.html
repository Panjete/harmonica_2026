    How notes work & the map

    The underlying logic behind notes, octaves, letters A–G, and why your 10-hole harmonica is laid out this way.

    The Physics

    1. Sound is just frequency

    Every musical note is a sound wave vibrating at a specific speed (frequency in Hertz):

    • Slow vibrations (~260 Hz) sound deep and low (Hole 1 on the left).
    • Fast vibrations (~2100 Hz) sound crisp and high (Hole 10 on the right).

    Your ear hears pitch logarithmically: whenever frequency doubles (e.g. 261 Hz → 523 Hz), your brain perceives it as the same note, just in a higher register. That doubling distance is called an Octave.

    The Alphabet

    2. The 7 repeating letters

    Western music took that frequency doubling and divided it into 7 primary steps, named alphabetically in an endless loop (tap any note to hear it):

    → → → → → → →

    Think of it as a circular buffer: after G, it loops back to A at a higher frequency. The highlighted notes (C, E, G) are your blow notes.

    The Math

    3. Why is it a “C” Harmonica?

    The universal cheerful melody pattern (the Major Scale: “Do Re Mi Fa Sol La Ti Do”) requires specific mathematical step sizes. If you start on C, all 7 steps land precisely on clean alphabet letters with zero sharps (♯) or flats (♭).

    Your C harmonica only contains these exact natural notes. There are no “wrong” keys inside. Every hole you blow or draw belongs to the C major family.

    Instrument Anatomy

    4. The 3 zones of your 10 holes

    Holes 1 – 3

    The Rhythm Engine

    Designed for chords. Blowing gives C – E – G (the home C chord). Drawing gives D – G – B (the walking G chord). Used for chugging train rhythms.

    Holes 4 – 7

    The Complete Melody Scale

    Contains the entire 8-note Do-Re-Mi scale in order: 4 blow (C), 4 draw (D), 5 blow (E), 5 draw (F), 6 blow (G), 6 draw (A), 7 draw (B), 7 blow (C). Almost all familiar songs live right here.

    Holes 8 – 10

    The High Register

    Higher octave repeats of the scale for bright accents, folk whistles, and soaring lead solos.

    The Physics of Breath

    5. Breathing in vs Breathing out

    Unlike a piano or guitar where you pluck or strike, the harmonica is an acoustic extension of your lungs:

    Blow (Exhale)

    Every single blow hole produces a note from the C Major Chord (C, E, or G). It sounds peaceful, grounded, and feels like "home".

    Draw (Inhale)

    Draw notes produce the intermediate walking steps (D, F, A, B). They feel active, longing, and want to resolve back to a blow note.
